Meridian Financial Services Ltd
SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING (SAR) PROCEDURE
Document Owner: MLRO Office | Version 1.8 | Last Reviewed: July 2025

1. Purpose
This procedure sets out how internal suspicions of money laundering or terrorist financing are reported and escalated to the National Crime Agency (NCA).

2. Internal Reporting
2.1 Any employee with knowledge or suspicion of money laundering must submit an internal report to the MLRO using the internal SAR form, regardless of transaction value.
2.2 Reports must be submitted as soon as reasonably practicable, and in any case within 24 hours of the suspicion arising.

3. MLRO Assessment
The MLRO reviews each internal report and decides within 2 business days whether it meets the threshold for an external SAR to the NCA.

4. External Submission
Where an external SAR is required, it is submitted via the NCA's SAR Online portal. Consent SARs (where the firm wishes to proceed with a transaction) require NCA consent or the expiry of the statutory notice period before proceeding.

5. Confidentiality
Details of a SAR, including its existence, must not be disclosed to the customer or any unauthorised person ("tipping off" offence under POCA 2002).

6. Record Keeping
All internal and external SAR records are retained for 5 years in a restricted-access system, separate from general customer records.

7. Quality Review
The MLRO conducts a quarterly quality review of a sample of SAR decisions to ensure consistency and identify training needs.
